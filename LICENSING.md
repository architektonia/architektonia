# Architektonia Research Institute — Licensing Policy

**Document:** `LICENSING.md`  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Repository:** `architektonia`  
**Status:** Public  
**Scope:** Public institutional repository

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the initial licensing principles applicable to materials published by the **Architektonia Research Institute** through this repository.

The Institute supports scientific openness, reproducibility, criticism, citation, and the dissemination of knowledge.

However, public accessibility does not automatically constitute an open-source, open-content, technological, or commercial license.

The Institute therefore distinguishes between:

```text
PUBLIC ACCESS
OPEN SCIENCE
OPEN CONTENT
OPEN SOURCE
TECHNOLOGICAL EXPLOITATION RIGHTS
```

These concepts are not equivalent.

---

## 2. General Principle

The publication of material in a public Architektonia repository does not, by itself, grant unrestricted rights to reproduce, modify, redistribute, commercialize, implement, or technologically exploit that material.

Licensing decisions are made according to the nature, scientific status, institutional status, confidentiality, security implications, and technological significance of each artifact.

The governing sequence is:

```text
CLASSIFICATION
      ↓
PUBLICATION DECISION
      ↓
LICENSING DECISION
      ↓
PUBLIC RELEASE
```

A licensing decision must therefore follow, not precede, appropriate classification and publication review.

---

## 3. No Repository-Wide Open License

At version v0.1, the `architektonia` repository is **not released under a repository-wide open-source or open-content license**.

Unless an explicit license is attached to a particular artifact, directory, publication, dataset, software package, or other material, no additional permission for reuse should be inferred solely from its public availability.

The repository primarily serves as the **public institutional interface of the Architektonia Research Institute**.

---

## 4. Scientific Openness

Architektonia intends to make qualified scientific knowledge publicly accessible whenever scientific, ethical, legal, confidentiality, intellectual-property, and security requirements permit.

Materials approved for open scientific dissemination may include:

- scientific publications;
- research reports;
- methodological documentation;
- reproducibility materials;
- selected scientific models;
- selected datasets;
- educational materials;
- scientific software; and
- other research outputs.

Where appropriate, such materials may receive an explicit open license.

Public scientific and documentary works may, for example, be released under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)** when explicitly designated.

Public software may, for example, be released under a recognized open-source software license such as **Apache License 2.0** or **MIT**, following an explicit licensing decision.

No such license applies automatically.

---

## 5. Different Artifacts May Have Different Licenses

Architektonia does not assume that a single license is appropriate for all forms of intellectual production.

Different licensing regimes may apply to:

```text
SCIENTIFIC PUBLICATIONS
SCIENTIFIC DOCUMENTATION
DATASETS
SOFTWARE
EDUCATIONAL MATERIAL
INSTITUTIONAL DOCUMENTS
RESEARCH INSTRUMENTS
TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS
TECHNOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS
```

A license applicable to one category must not be interpreted as applying to another category unless explicitly stated.

In particular:

```text
OPEN SCIENCE
        ≠
OPEN SOFTWARE
        ≠
OPEN TECHNOLOGY
```

---

## 6. Protection of Technological Foundations — BT (Bases Technologiques)

The Institute recognizes a class of technological assets identified as:

**BT — Technological Foundations/Bases Technologiques**

BT may include technological knowledge, architectures, methods, implementations, systems, processes, or other assets capable of supporting operational or commercial technologies.

BT is **protected by default**.

BT material must not be considered openly licensed merely because related scientific concepts, institutional principles, research findings, or public documentation have been published.

Depending on the nature of the asset, protection may include:

- confidentiality;
- restricted access;
- copyright;
- proprietary licensing;
- trade-secret protection where applicable;
- patent protection where appropriate;
- contractual restrictions; and
- other appropriate intellectual-property mechanisms.

---

## 7. BT-Enabling Information

Protection is determined not only by the formal classification or documentary family of an artifact, but also by what the information contained within that artifact could materially enable.

Information outside the BT documentary category may nevertheless contribute materially to the:

- reconstruction;
- implementation;
- replication;
- operationalization;
- optimization; or
- commercial exploitation

of a protected technological foundation.

Such information is referred to in this policy as **BT-enabling information**.

The Institute therefore adopts the following principle:

```text
BT
+
BT-ENABLING INFORMATION
        ↓
PROTECTED BY DEFAULT
```

An artifact must not be considered safe for open licensing solely because it is not formally classified as BT.

---

## 8. Publication Does Not Override Protection

Scientific openness does not require the disclosure of every technical mechanism used to produce, implement, or operationalize scientific knowledge.

The Institute may therefore publish:

```text
SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLES
RESEARCH RESULTS
VALIDATED MODELS
OBSERVATIONS
METHODS
```

while withholding:

```text
PROTECTED IMPLEMENTATIONS
SECURITY-SENSITIVE DETAILS
PROPRIETARY TECHNOLOGICAL ARCHITECTURES
CONFIDENTIAL DATA
BT
BT-ENABLING INFORMATION
```

where disclosure would conflict with legitimate scientific, ethical, security, confidentiality, institutional, or intellectual-property requirements.

---

## 9. Software

Software developed within Architektonia is not automatically open source.

Before public release, software should be evaluated for:

- scientific value;
- reproducibility value;
- security implications;
- dependencies;
- confidential information;
- embedded credentials or secrets;
- institutional intellectual property;
- patent implications;
- BT content; and
- BT-enabling content.

Only software explicitly approved for open-source publication should receive an open-source license.

Possible licenses may include **Apache License 2.0**, **MIT**, or another license selected for the specific project.

Private or protected source code remains outside this open-source licensing regime.

---

## 10. Scientific and Documentary Content

Scientific publications and public research documentation may receive an explicit Creative Commons license when appropriate.

The Institute may use **CC BY 4.0** for material intentionally released for broad scientific reuse, adaptation, citation, and redistribution.

Institutional documents are not automatically subject to the same licensing regime.

A document may be publicly readable while remaining subject to ordinary copyright protection unless a broader license is explicitly granted.

Therefore:

```text
PUBLICLY READABLE
        ≠
OPENLY LICENSED
```

---

## 11. Datasets

Datasets require independent licensing and publication review.

Before release, considerations may include:

- participant confidentiality;
- personal information;
- consent;
- research ethics;
- contractual obligations;
- third-party rights;
- re-identification risk;
- security implications;
- intellectual-property implications; and
- technological sensitivity.

No dataset should be assumed to inherit the license of the repository, associated software, or publication.

---

## 12. Confidential and Restricted Material

Confidential, restricted, security-sensitive, or otherwise protected material is outside the scope of public licensing.

This includes, where applicable:

- confidential research data;
- identifiable participant information;
- private experimental records;
- credentials;
- authentication material;
- secrets;
- operational access information;
- protected security configurations;
- restricted institutional information;
- protected source code;
- BT; and
- BT-enabling information.

Such material should not be placed in this public repository.

---

## 13. Explicit Licensing Requirement

An open license should be considered valid for Architektonia material only when the applicable license is explicitly identified for the relevant artifact or defined scope.

Licensing may therefore occur at the level of a:

```text
REPOSITORY
DIRECTORY
SOFTWARE PACKAGE
DATASET
PUBLICATION
DOCUMENT
ARTIFACT
```

The applicable scope should be clearly stated.

Where multiple licensing regimes coexist within a repository, their respective boundaries should be documented explicitly.

---

## 14. Attribution and Third-Party Material

Architektonia may incorporate, reference, analyze, or build upon material created by third parties.

Third-party material remains subject to its own copyright, licensing, attribution, contractual, or other applicable conditions.

Architektonia does not claim ownership of third-party intellectual property merely because such material is referenced or used within its research activities.

Where third-party material is redistributed, its applicable licensing and attribution requirements should be preserved.

---

## 15. Licensing Matrix

The Institute's initial licensing orientation is:

| Artifact Class | Default Position |
|---|---|
| Public scientific publications | Explicit open license may be applied |
| Public scientific documentation | Explicit open license may be applied |
| Public educational material | Explicit open license may be applied |
| Public scientific software | Project-specific open-source license may be applied |
| Public datasets | Dataset-specific decision required |
| Public institutional material | Copyright by default unless explicitly licensed |
| Private experimental material | Not publicly licensed |
| Confidential or restricted material | Not published |
| Security-sensitive material | Not published by default |
| BT | Protected by default |
| BT-enabling information | Protected by default |

---

## 16. Core Licensing Rule

Architektonia adopts the following general rule:

```text
OPEN SCIENCE
    → OPEN LICENSE WHEN EXPLICITLY APPROVED

OPEN SOFTWARE
    → OPEN-SOURCE LICENSE WHEN EXPLICITLY APPROVED

PUBLIC INSTITUTIONAL MATERIAL
    → COPYRIGHT UNLESS OTHERWISE SPECIFIED

BT
    → PROTECTED BY DEFAULT

BT-ENABLING INFORMATION
    → PROTECTED BY DEFAULT

CONFIDENTIAL / RESTRICTED MATERIAL
    → NOT PUBLIC
```

The fundamental distinction is:

> **Public access does not constitute permission for unrestricted technological exploitation.**

---

This document represents the Institute's initial public licensing policy.

It may evolve as Architektonia develops formal frameworks for:

- scientific publication;
- intellectual-property management;
- technological transfer;
- open-source software;
- research data governance;

- protected technological foundations;
- institutional documentation; and
- collaboration with external researchers and organizations.

Changes to this policy should be versioned and publicly traceable.
