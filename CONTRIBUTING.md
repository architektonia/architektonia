# Contributing to the Architektonia Research Institute

**Document:** `CONTRIBUTING.md`  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Repository:** `architektonia`  
**Status:** Public  
**Function:** Early-stage contributor recruitment, integration, and development

---

# 1. Purpose

The Architektonia Research Institute is currently building its first circle of scientific and institutional contributors.

At this stage, the Institute does **not** seek to maximize the number of contributions it receives.

It seeks to develop a small and progressively expanding community of people capable of helping its scientific mission flourish.

The current contribution model is therefore based on three conditions:

```text
WANTS THE MISSION TO FLOURISH
              +
IS WILLING TO SEEK TRUTH
              +
CAN CONTRIBUTE CONSTRUCTIVELY
              ↓
POTENTIAL ARCHITEKTONIA CONTRIBUTOR
```

These conditions are intentionally relational.

Scientific competence matters.

So do reliability, intellectual independence, capacity for cooperation, respect for institutional boundaries, and the ability to contribute without overwhelming the Institute's present capacity to integrate new work.

The objective is not:

```text
MAXIMUM CONTRIBUTORS
```

It is:

```text
SUSTAINABLE SCIENTIFIC COOPERATION
```

---

# 2. Growth Must Follow Integration Capacity

Every contributor introduces potential scientific capacity.

Every contributor also requires some degree of communication, orientation, review, coordination, access management, institutional memory, and relationship.

Contribution therefore creates both:

```text
NEW CAPACITY
      +
NEW INTEGRATION LOAD
```

An Institute that receives contributions faster than it can responsibly integrate them may become less capable rather than more capable.

Architektonia therefore adopts the following early-stage principle:

> **Grow the circle only as fast as the circle can responsibly receive new people.**

This means that openness should expand progressively with the Institute's capacity to support it.

```text
CAPACITY
      ↓
OPENNESS
      ↓
INTEGRATION
      ↓
LEARNING
      ↓
GREATER CAPACITY
      ↓
GREATER OPENNESS
```

---

# 3. What Architektonia Currently Seeks

At this stage, Architektonia seeks **people before volume**.

The Institute is especially interested in potential contributors who demonstrate some combination of:

- genuine interest in the scientific mission;
- intellectual curiosity;
- willingness to investigate reality wherever the evidence may lead;
- capacity for rigorous disagreement;
- intellectual humility;
- respect for human dignity;
- scientific, technical, professional, or practical competence relevant to the work;
- reliability;
- capacity to collaborate;
- willingness to learn the Institute's emerging architecture;
- remarkable respect for confidentiality and institutional boundaries;
- capacity to work without creating unnecessary organizational burden; and
- willingness to help build the Institute rather than merely submit material to it.

No single contributor is expected to possess all relevant competencies.

The purpose of a contributor circle is precisely to bring different competencies into relationship.

---

# 4. Mission Alignment Without Intellectual Conformity

Architektonia seeks contributors who want its mission to flourish.

This does **not** mean that contributors must agree with Architektonia's scientific propositions.

> **Contribution does not require agreement with Architektonia's theses.**

Mission alignment means being willing to help the Institute investigate its scientific questions faithfully.

It does not mean defending its existing answers.

A contributor remains free to challenge:

- observations;
- assumptions;
- hypotheses;
- definitions;
- methods;
- models;
- evidence;
- interpretations;
- inferences;
- conclusions; and
- Architektonia's own scientific frameworks.

The Institute should never build its trusted circle by selecting only people who agree with it.

```text
MISSION ALIGNMENT
        +
INTELLECTUAL INDEPENDENCE
        =
HEALTHY SCIENTIFIC COLLABORATION
```

A trusted contributor may sometimes be the person most capable of telling the Institute that it is wrong.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> **Do you agree with Architektonia?**

It is:

> **Do you want to help Architektonia investigate its scientific questions faithfully?**

---

# 5. Progressive Contribution Path

Early contribution to Architektonia should normally develop progressively.

```text
DISCOVERY
    ↓
MISSION ALIGNMENT
    ↓
EXPRESSION OF INTEREST
    ↓
INITIAL CONVERSATION
    ↓
SMALL COLLABORATION
    ↓
MUTUAL EVALUATION
    ↓
CONTRIBUTOR
    ↓
TRUSTED CONTRIBUTOR
    ↓
POSSIBLE EXPANDED RESPONSIBILITY
```

This process is relational rather than merely transactional.

A person does not become a trusted contributor simply by submitting a large amount of work.

Trust develops through repeated interaction, demonstrated competence, scientific integrity, reliability, and responsible participation.

---

## 5.1 Stage 1 — Discovery

A potential contributor first discovers the Institute and its mission.

At this stage, the appropriate first step is to become familiar with the public work of Architektonia.

Potential contributors should understand at least:

- what the Institute studies;
- why the Institute exists;
- its general scientific orientation;
- its commitment to evidence;
- its commitment to human dignity; and
- the distinction between scientific exploration and institutional authority.

No commitment is expected at this stage.

```text
DISCOVER
      ↓
READ
      ↓
UNDERSTAND
```

---

## 5.2 Stage 2 — Mission Alignment

A person interested in contributing should determine whether they can genuinely support the mission of the Institute.

This does not require agreement with Architektonia's theses.

A potential contributor should be able to disagree with an Architektonia proposition while still wanting the Institute's search for truth to succeed.

At this stage, three questions are particularly important:

```text
DO I WANT THIS MISSION TO FLOURISH?

AM I WILLING TO FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE
EVEN WHEN IT CHALLENGES MY EXPECTATIONS?

CAN I CONTRIBUTE IN A WAY THAT HELPS
RATHER THAN OVERWHELMS THE WORK?
```

---

## 5.3 Stage 3 — Expression of Interest

A person who sees a possible fit should normally begin by **expressing interest**, rather than immediately producing or submitting substantial work.

The purpose is simple:

```text
INTEREST
      ↓
CONVERSATION
      ↓
BOUNDED OPPORTUNITY
      ↓
CONTRIBUTION
```

This protects both the potential contributor and the Institute from investing substantial effort in work that cannot presently be integrated.

An expression of interest may identify:

- why the mission is of interest;
- relevant experience or competencies;
- areas of scientific interest;
- possible ways of contributing; and
- questions about the Institute.

At this stage, a concise and thoughtful introduction is generally more useful than a large unsolicited contribution.

---

## 5.4 Stage 4 — Initial Conversation

Before substantial collaboration begins, Architektonia normally seeks a direct conversation with the potential contributor.

The purpose is **mutual discovery**.

Topics may include:

- the person's interest in Architektonia;
- relevant experience or expertise;
- scientific interests;
- possible contribution areas;
- expectations;
- available time;
- preferred modes of collaboration;
- relevant boundaries;
- potential conflicts of interest; and
- questions about the Institute.

This conversation is not intended to function merely as a conventional employment interview.

Both sides are evaluating whether a productive scientific relationship may exist.

```text
ARCHITEKTONIA
      ↕
POTENTIAL CONTRIBUTOR
      ↓
MUTUAL DISCOVERY
```

---

## 5.5 Stage 5 — Small Collaboration

Where mutual interest exists, the preferred next step is a **small, bounded contribution**.

Examples may include:

- reviewing a short scientific document;
- criticizing a hypothesis;
- reproducing a limited analysis;
- reviewing a method;
- examining a model;
- testing a piece of software;
- improving documentation;
- conducting a limited literature review; or
- participating in a bounded research discussion.

The first collaboration should normally be:

```text
SMALL
BOUNDED
OBSERVABLE
REVERSIBLE
USEFUL
```

The purpose is not to test personal worth.

It is to discover how the collaboration actually functions.

---

## 5.6 Stage 6 — Mutual Evaluation

After an initial collaboration, both Architektonia and the potential contributor should evaluate the experience.

Relevant questions may include:

```text
Did the collaboration create scientific value?

Was communication workable?

Was disagreement handled constructively?

Were commitments respected?

Was confidentiality respected?

Were institutional boundaries respected?

Was the integration burden proportionate
to the value created?

Did both sides learn something?

Does continued collaboration make sense?
```

The question of **integration burden** matters.

A technically excellent contribution may still require more coordination than the Institute can presently sustain.

Likewise, a modest contribution may be extremely valuable if it increases the capacity of others.

A successful first collaboration does not automatically require continued collaboration.

An imperfect first collaboration does not automatically prevent it.

The objective is to observe the emerging relationship faithfully.

---

## 5.7 Stage 7 — Contributor

Where collaboration proves mutually valuable, a person may become a recurring Architektonia contributor.

A contributor may participate in defined:

- research activities;
- repositories;
- reviews;
- discussions;
- experiments;
- documentation;
- software development; or
- other authorized work.

Access and responsibilities should remain proportionate to:

- demonstrated need;
- relevant competence;
- established trust;
- information sensitivity;
- research requirements; and
- the Institute's capacity to support the collaboration.

Contributor status does not automatically confer institutional authority.

```text
CONTRIBUTION
      ≠
AUTHORITY
```

---

## 5.8 Stage 8 — Trusted Contributor

Over time, some contributors may develop deeper responsibilities.

Trust should emerge from observable experience rather than assumption.

Relevant evidence may include:

- sustained scientific integrity;
- reliability;
- responsible handling of disagreement;
- protection of confidential information;
- sound judgment;
- demonstrated competence;
- constructive collaboration;
- respect for institutional boundaries;
- ability to help other contributors;
- contribution to the Institute's capacity; and
- demonstrated commitment to the mission.

Trusted contributors may eventually help review, accompany, mentor, or integrate newer contributors.

This represents an important institutional transition.

```text
TRUSTED CONTRIBUTOR
        ↓
CAN HELP RECEIVE
        ↓
NEW CONTRIBUTORS
        ↓
INCREASED INTEGRATION CAPACITY
        ↓
RESPONSIBLE GROWTH
```

The contributor architecture can therefore gradually become self-supporting.

---

## 5.9 Stage 9 — Expanded Responsibility

Some trusted contributors may eventually assume greater scientific, technical, mentoring, reviewing, coordinating, or institutional responsibilities.

Expanded responsibility should follow demonstrated capacity and legitimate institutional need.

It should not emerge merely from longevity, proximity to the founder, volume of contributions, or informal influence.

Where expanded responsibility includes institutional authority, that authority must arise through the appropriate governance process.

```text
TRUST
      +
COMPETENCE
      +
LEGITIMATE NEED
      +
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORIZATION
      ↓
EXPANDED RESPONSIBILITY
```

# 6. Growth Through Concentric Circles

Architektonia should grow through **concentric circles of trust and responsibility**, rather than through uncontrolled accumulation of contributors.

```text
              ┌───────────────────────────┐
              │   PUBLIC COMMUNITY        │
              │                           │
              │   ┌───────────────────┐   │
              │   │ CONTRIBUTORS      │   │
              │   │                   │   │
              │   │   ┌───────────┐   │   │
              │   │   │ TRUSTED   │   │   │
              │   │   │ CIRCLE    │   │   │
              │   │   └───────────┘   │   │
              │   │                   │   │
              │   └───────────────────┘   │
              │                           │
              └───────────────────────────┘
```

Movement toward the center corresponds to greater established trust, responsibility, access, and capacity to support others.

It does not represent greater human worth.

```text
GREATER TRUST
      ≠
GREATER HUMAN WORTH
```

People may also make scientifically valuable contributions without ever entering the closest contributor circle.

---

# 7. Contribution Capacity Before Expansion

Architektonia should normally expand its contributor base only when its capacity to integrate contributors has also expanded.

Indicators of increased integration capacity may include:

- trusted contributors able to accompany newcomers;
- functioning review processes;
- clear repository boundaries;
- documented research methods;
- manageable review workload;
- established communication channels;
- appropriate access controls;
- scientific qualification processes;
- sufficient institutional memory; and
- adequate time for meaningful collaboration.

This creates a deliberate growth mechanism:

```text
BUILD CAPACITY
      ↓
OPEN A LITTLE
      ↓
INTEGRATE
      ↓
LEARN
      ↓
BUILD MORE CAPACITY
      ↓
OPEN FURTHER
```

Architektonia should not maximize contributor count.

It should maximize the **sustainable capacity for meaningful scientific cooperation**.

---

# 8. Contribution Invitations

During the early development of the Institute, substantial contributions may be primarily:

- invited;
- discussed in advance;
- initiated through an existing relationship; or
- accepted after an initial conversation.

Unsolicited interest is welcome.

**Unsolicited large contributions are discouraged unless a repository explicitly invites them.**

A person interested in contributing should therefore normally begin by expressing interest rather than producing substantial work without coordination.

This is not intended to create intellectual closure.

It is a capacity boundary.

```text
OPENNESS TO PEOPLE
        +
LIMITED INTEGRATION CAPACITY
        ↓
PROGRESSIVE ADMISSION
```

# 10. Conduct

All contributors are subject to `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` within its applicable scope.

The governing principle remains:

> **Attack the idea fearlessly. Be remarkably kind to the person.**

Strong disagreement is compatible with close scientific collaboration.

Personal degradation is not.

---

# 11. Confidentiality, Security, and Access

Becoming a contributor does not automatically provide access to:

- private repositories;
- confidential research;
- participant data;
- experimental infrastructure;
- institutional systems;
- credentials;
- security-sensitive information;
- protected technological foundations; or
- other restricted resources.

Access should be granted progressively according to legitimate need, demonstrated trust, applicable authority, and information classification.

```text
RELATIONSHIP
      ↓
TRUST
      ↓
LEGITIMATE NEED
      ↓
AUTHORIZED ACCESS
```

Access is not a reward.

It is a capability granted for a defined purpose.

A trusted relationship does not, by itself, create a legitimate need for access.

Sensitive or security-related contributions may be subject to additional procedures.

---

# 12. Contribution Does Not Create Authority

Contributor status does not automatically confer:

- scientific authority;
- institutional authority;
- governance authority;
- publication authority;
- access authority;
- decision authority; or
- authority to represent the Institute.

```text
CONTRIBUTOR
      ≠
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
```

Likewise:

```text
TRUST
      ≠
UNLIMITED AUTHORITY
```

Any additional authority must arise through the appropriate institutional process.

---

# 13. Capacity Limits

Architektonia may temporarily pause or limit recruitment even when excellent potential contributors are available.

Possible reasons include:

- insufficient mentoring capacity;
- excessive review workload;
- major institutional development work;
- inadequate documentation;
- insufficient technical infrastructure;
- security constraints;
- research priorities;
- lack of appropriate supervision; or
- excessive concentration of responsibility in too few people.

A temporary inability to receive a contributor should not be interpreted as rejection of that person's potential value.

Sometimes the responsible institutional answer is simply:

> **Not yet.**

---

# 14. Future Opening

The contribution architecture described in this v0.1 reflects an early-stage Institute.

As Architektonia develops sufficient scientific, human, technical, and institutional capacity, its contribution model may progressively become more open.

A future mature contribution framework may support:

- independent pull requests;
- broader public scientific submissions;
- external replication;
- open peer review;
- distributed maintainership;
- formal reviewer roles;
- public research proposals;
- structured dataset contributions;
- external software contributions; and
- larger interdisciplinary contributor communities.

At that stage, contribution requirements will increasingly focus on the **quality and qualification of contributions themselves**.

The fundamental principle will nevertheless remain:

> **Contribution does not require agreement with Architektonia's theses.**

# 16. Evolution of This Framework

This document represents the initial contribution framework of the Architektonia Research Institute.

It is intentionally designed for an Institute whose contributor circle and integration capacity are still small.

As the Institute grows, this document should evolve with its actual capacity rather than imitate the contribution model of a mature institution.

```text
CAPACITY
      ↓
OPENNESS
      ↓
INTEGRATION
      ↓
LEARNING
      ↓
GREATER CAPACITY
```

Changes to this framework should be versioned and publicly traceable.

The contribution architecture itself remains open to observation, criticism, testing, and improvement.
