# MISSION

The Architektonia Research Institute is an independent research institute dedicated to the scientific study of the emergence, transformation, stabilization, maturation, repair, and disorganization of human communion.

Its mission is to investigate how persons and human groups — including individuals (internal architecture), couples, families, teams, organizations, communities, institutions, and states — develop structures that enable or hinder durable forms of cooperation, trust, shared life, and collective flourishing.

Architektonia seeks to identify observable regularities, develop testable models, construct appropriate scientific instruments, and progressively formulate a rigorous science of human architectures.

The Institute begins from a simple methodological commitment:

Reality precedes our models of reality.

Its models, concepts, and institutional structures must therefore remain open to correction through observation, experimentation, comparison, and scientific criticism.

# RESEARCH DOMAINS

Architektonia investigates human systems across multiple scales, from interpersonal relationships to complex institutions.

Its principal research domains include:

Human communion and cooperation — conditions under which persons and groups become capable of sustained common action.

Trust — emergence, stabilization, degradation, loss, and restoration of trust.

Human architectures — structures, rules, roles, practices, environments, and relationships that influence collective behavior.

Disorganization — processes through which human systems lose coherence, coordination, resilience, or capacity for common action.

Transformation — mechanisms through which knowledge, experience, capability, and institutional structure produce changes in human systems.

Repair and resilience — conditions under which damaged relationships, organizations, or institutions can recover.

Institutional development — emergence, stabilization, maturation, and transformation of organizations and institutions.

Scientific and computational modeling — formal, statistical, computational, and simulation-based approaches to studying complex human systems.

The Institute is inherently interdisciplinary and may draw upon relevant work in the social sciences, natural sciences, engineering, mathematics, philosophy, computer science, and other fields where they contribute to the investigation of its research questions.

# SCIENTIFIC APPROACH

Architektonia seeks to distinguish carefully between:

**observation → interpretation → hypothesis → model → test → result → scientific claim**

The Institute does not assume that its initial conceptual framework constitutes an established science.

Instead, its purpose is to determine which concepts can be operationalized, which phenomena can be measured, which regularities can be reproduced, and which proposed relationships survive systematic confrontation with reality.

Its scientific approach therefore emphasizes:

empirical observation;

explicit hypotheses;

traceable sources and provenance;

reproducibility where possible;

comparison across cases;

quantitative and qualitative methods;

multivariate and statistical analysis;

computational modeling and simulation;

explicit representation of uncertainty;

falsifiability and contradiction;

separation between exploratory results and established institutional or scientific claims.

Architektonia also develops methods for preserving the history of scientific reasoning so that conclusions can be traced back through the observations, transformations, assumptions, models, and decisions from which they emerged.

# RESEARCH PROGRAMS

The Institute's initial research agenda is organized around five interconnected programs.

## P1 — Trust

Study the emergence, maintenance, degradation, loss, and restoration of trust within human systems.

## P2 — Disorganization

Identify mechanisms and recurring patterns through which relationships, groups, organizations, and institutions become progressively disorganized.

## P3 — Transformation

Investigate how perceived or established truths, lived experiences, knowledge, and capabilities become forces capable of transforming human systems.

## P4 — Architectures and Outcomes

Study how different architectures (declared or effective) — including roles, rules, structures, practices, incentives, boundaries, and environments — influence observable outcomes.

## P5 — Repair

Investigate the conditions, processes, and architectures through which damaged human systems may regain coherence, trust, functionality, and capacity for common action.

These programs are expected to evolve as empirical evidence accumulates and the Institute's scientific instruments mature.

# Human–AI Relations

Artificial intelligence introduces a new class of actors, instruments, and mediating systems into human environments.

The **Architektonia Research Institute** therefore maintains a dedicated research program on the relationships between humans, artificial intelligence systems, and the human architectures within which these interactions occur.

This program does **not** redefine the Institute's general mission as the study of human–AI relations. Rather, it recognizes that increasingly capable artificial intelligence systems may significantly affect the emergence, transformation, stabilization, maturation, repair, or disorganization of human systems.

### P6 — Human–AI Relations

The program investigates how interactions between humans and artificial intelligence systems affect persons, relationships, groups, organizations, communities, and institutions.

Research questions may include:

- how humans attribute competence, intention, authority, trustworthiness, personality, or agency to artificial intelligence systems;
- how repeated interaction with AI influences human judgment, decision-making, learning, autonomy, responsibility, and behavior;
- how anthropomorphic AI systems affect attachment, trust, dependence, persuasion, cooperation, and interpersonal relationships;
- how AI systems mediate relationships between humans;
- how human authority should relate to increasingly autonomous artificial agents;
- how responsibility and accountability should be distributed when humans and artificial agents participate in the same decision or action chain;
- how artificial agents should be bounded by institutional architectures, permissions, authority structures, and mechanisms of human governance;
- how multiple artificial agents interacting with humans and with one another may alter organizational and institutional dynamics;
- under what conditions AI systems strengthen existing human architectures, create new ones, or contribute to their disorganization; and
- what forms of technical and institutional architecture preserve meaningful human agency and governance in environments containing increasingly capable artificial intelligence.

The program distinguishes between at least three levels of analysis:

```text
HUMAN
   ↕
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

            ↓

HUMAN ↔ AI ↔ HUMAN

            ↓

HUMANS + AI SYSTEMS
        ↕
ORGANIZATIONS / INSTITUTIONS
```

The first concerns the direct relationship between a person and an artificial intelligence system.

The second concerns artificial intelligence as a **mediator of relationships between humans**.

The third concerns environments in which humans and artificial agents coexist within broader organizational or institutional architectures.

### Scientific Orientation

The Institute does not assume that artificial intelligence systems are persons, moral agents, or equivalent to human participants.

Terms such as *agent*, *agency*, *autonomy*, *role*, *authority*, *trust*, or *relationship* must therefore be defined operationally and distinguished carefully from anthropomorphic interpretations.

Research should distinguish, where relevant, between:

```text
PERCEIVED AGENCY
        ≠
TECHNICAL CAPABILITY
        ≠
DELEGATED AUTHORITY
        ≠
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
        ≠
MORAL AGENCY
```

This distinction is particularly important as artificial intelligence systems become more capable of persistent action, tool use, planning, adaptation, communication, and participation in complex human environments.

The purpose of the program is therefore not merely to study artificial intelligence as a technology, but to investigate scientifically the **new relational and architectural phenomena that emerge when artificial systems become active participants or mediators within human systems**.

### Relationship to the Institute's Other Research Programs

P6 is transversal to the Institute's other research programs.

Human–AI systems may affect:

**P1 — Trust**  
How trust develops between humans and artificial systems, and how AI affects trust between humans.

**P2 — Disorganization**  
How artificial systems may amplify, reduce, or introduce mechanisms of disorganization.

**P3 — Transformation**  
How AI changes access to knowledge, capability, interpretation, and the ability of persons or groups to act.

**P4 — Architectures and Outcomes**  
How institutional and technical architectures governing AI influence observable human outcomes.

**P5 — Repair**  
Whether and under what conditions artificial intelligence can assist — or interfere with — the repair of damaged human systems.

P6 therefore constitutes a distinct research program while remaining connected to the Institute's broader scientific investigation of human architectures.

# TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

Architektonia is developing a research architecture that separates different functions of scientific and institutional work.

At a high level, the architecture distinguishes three environments:

ENV-1
Computation · Simulation · Experimental Agents
            │
            ▼
ENV-2
Exploration · Scientific Objects · Experimental Memory
            │
            ▼
ENV-3
Institutionalization · Documentation · Human Governance

These environments have different purposes, authority boundaries, persistence requirements, and security constraints.

Information is not assumed to move freely between them. Interfaces and transitions are designed to preserve provenance, review, authorization, and appropriate human control.

The architecture is being developed progressively and remains subject to scientific and technical validation.

Detailed experimental infrastructure, security mechanisms, credentials, restricted configurations, and non-public operational information are intentionally excluded from this public repository.

# PUBLICATIONS

Architektonia intends to make qualified research outputs publicly accessible whenever scientific, ethical, legal, confidentiality, and security requirements permit.

Public outputs may eventually include:

research papers;

preprints;

scientific reports;

methodological papers;

public datasets;

models;

simulation results;

research protocols;

reproducibility materials;

technical specifications;

scientific software; and

institutional research reports.

Publication does not imply that every stage of research is public.

Exploratory work, confidential data, security-sensitive material, unqualified hypotheses, and research involving protected information may remain within controlled environments.

Public scientific claims should be distinguishable from exploratory propositions and should provide sufficient provenance to understand the basis on which they are made.

# GOVERNANCE

The Architektonia Research Institute distinguishes between scientific exploration, institutional authority, and public publication.

Experimental systems, computational agents, research hypotheses, or exploratory outputs do not acquire institutional authority merely because they have been generated, stored, analyzed, or recommended by a technical system.

Institutional authority remains subject to defined human governance processes.

The Institute also seeks to maintain explicit boundaries between:

EXPLORATION

      ↓

QUALIFICATION

      ↓

INSTITUTIONALIZATION

      ↓

PUBLICATION


These distinctions are intended to preserve scientific freedom while preventing exploratory artifacts from being mistaken for validated scientific conclusions or authoritative institutional positions.

More detailed governance documents may be published separately when formally adopted and approved for public dissemination.
