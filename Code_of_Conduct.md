# Architektonia Research Institute — Code of Conduct

**Document:** `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Repository:** `architektonia`  
**Status:** Public  
**Scope:** Research activities conducted within or through Architektonia repositories

---

## 1. The Architektonia Principle

> **Attack the idea fearlessly. Be remarkably kind to the person.**

Architektonia exists to investigate reality **wherever the evidence may lead**.

Serious scientific inquiry requires the freedom to question assumptions, challenge interpretations, criticize methods, contradict conclusions, test models, expose errors, and reject hypotheses when the evidence requires it.

At the same time, intellectual disagreement never removes the dignity of the person with whom we disagree, **nor does scientific inquiry remove the freedom of the person seeking truth**.

Architektonia therefore holds two commitments together:

```text
INTELLECTUAL RIGOR
        │
        ▼
Attack the idea fearlessly.

FREEDOM OF INQUIRY
        │
        ▼
Follow the evidence wherever it leads.

FREEDOM OF THE PERSON
        │
        ▼
Participation in inquiry does not surrender human agency.

HUMAN DIGNITY
        │
        ▼
Disagreement never diminishes the person. Be remarkably kind to the person.
```

These principles are mutually reinforcing.

The freedom to investigate does not confer authority over the person, and the freedom of the person does not place their ideas beyond scientific examination.

Neither principle cancels the other.

**Kindness does not require agreement.**

**Disagreement does not justify unkindness.**

---

## 2. Purpose

This Code establishes the conditions under which people participate in research activities conducted within or through repositories of the **Architektonia Research Institute**.

Its purpose is not to determine which scientific ideas are acceptable.

Its purpose is to establish an architecture in which people can pursue truth together through:

```text
OPEN INQUIRY
      +
RIGOROUS DISAGREEMENT
      +
SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY
      +
HUMAN DIGNITY
```

The Institute considers the coexistence of these conditions essential to serious scientific work.

---

## 3. Scientific Freedom and Disagreement

No scientific claim should be protected from good-faith examination merely because it is established, popular, institutionally supported, personally important, or associated with Architektonia itself.

Participants are free to question and critically examine:

- hypotheses;
- observations;
- interpretations;
- assumptions;
- definitions;
- methods;
- measurements;
- models;
- simulations;
- evidence;
- statistical analyses;
- causal claims;
- theoretical frameworks;
- scientific publications;
- institutional research positions; and
- conclusions produced by Architektonia.

Good-faith scientific criticism may be strong.

A participant may argue that an idea is unsupported, internally inconsistent, poorly defined, methodologically weak, contradicted by evidence, statistically unjustified, unfalsifiable, misleading, incorrect, or worthy of rejection.

Such criticism is not, by itself, misconduct.

Architektonia explicitly protects good-faith:

```text
QUESTIONING
CRITICISM
REPLICATION
CONTRADICTION
FALSIFICATION
REQUESTS FOR EVIDENCE
METHODOLOGICAL CHALLENGE
CORRECTION
```

Scientific disagreement must not be suppressed merely because it is uncomfortable.

Scientific freedom does not, however, provide permission to harass, humiliate, threaten, intimidate, or deliberately demean another person.

---

## 4. Critique Ideas, Not Human Worth

Architektonia distinguishes between criticism of a proposition and degradation of a person.

Within scientific inquiry, no claim, evidence, method, model, inference, or conclusion should be treated as immune from examination.

```text
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE CLAIM          ✓
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE EVIDENCE       ✓
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE METHOD         ✓
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE MODEL          ✓
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE INFERENCE      ✓
ALWAYS CHALLENGE THE CONCLUSION     ✓

NEVER DEGRADE THE PERSON            ✗
NEVER HARASS THE PERSON             ✗
NEVER THREATEN THE PERSON           ✗
NEVER HUMILIATE THE PERSON          ✗
NEVER INTIMIDATE THE PERSON         ✗
```

Here, **always challenge** does not mean that every participant must oppose every proposition.

It means that every scientific proposition must remain open to examination, testing, contradiction, and revision.

A person's error does not reduce that person's dignity.

A person's status does not protect that person's ideas from criticism.

Scientific authority, academic credentials, institutional position, seniority, popularity, or reputation must not make a proposition immune from examination.

Likewise, lack of status must not make a person unworthy of being heard.

> **Attack the idea fearlessly. Be remarkably kind to the person.**

---

## 5. Expected Conduct

Participants in Architektonia research activities are expected to act with intellectual honesty, scientific seriousness, and respect for persons.

Expected conduct includes:

- engaging arguments on their merits;
- distinguishing observation from interpretation;
- distinguishing hypotheses from established findings;
- acknowledging relevant uncertainty;
- representing opposing positions fairly;
- asking for evidence when appropriate;
- providing evidence when making substantive claims;
- correcting significant errors when they are identified;
- acknowledging legitimate criticism;
- giving appropriate attribution;
- identifying relevant conflicts of interest;
- avoiding deliberate distortion of another person's position;
- respecting confidentiality and legitimate information boundaries;
- allowing others to question one's own work;
- treating disagreement as a normal part of scientific inquiry; and
- maintaining respect for the dignity of other participants.

Participants should seek to understand a position before criticizing it.

Strong criticism should become **more precise**, not more personal.

---

## 6. Intellectual Humility

Architektonia recognizes that researchers, institutions, models, artificial intelligence systems, and scientific communities can be wrong.

Participants should remain capable of saying:

```text
I DO NOT KNOW.

THE EVIDENCE IS INSUFFICIENT.

MY HYPOTHESIS MAY BE WRONG.

THIS RESULT HAS NOT BEEN REPLICATED.

AN ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION EXISTS.

I HAVE CHANGED MY POSITION.

I MADE AN ERROR.
```

Changing one's conclusion because of better evidence is not intellectual weakness.

It is part of scientific integrity.

---

## 7. Unacceptable Conduct

The following behaviors are incompatible with responsible participation in Architektonia research activities:

- harassment;
- threats or incitement of violence;
- intimidation;
- sustained personal attacks;
- deliberate humiliation;
- discriminatory harassment;
- stalking;
- doxxing or unauthorized disclosure of personal information;
- deliberate misrepresentation of another participant's statements or work;
- plagiarism;
- knowingly false attribution;
- fabrication or deliberate falsification of evidence presented as genuine;
- retaliation against good-faith scientific criticism;
- retaliation against good-faith reporting of misconduct;
- abuse of institutional position to suppress legitimate scientific disagreement;
- deliberate disruption intended primarily to prevent others from participating;
- unauthorized disclosure of confidential or restricted information; and
- knowingly introducing credentials, secrets, protected security information, or other prohibited material into Architektonia repositories.

Disagreement, criticism, contradiction, requests for evidence, or rejection of an idea are not harassment merely because they are strongly expressed.

Context, persistence, severity, evidence, consequences, and behavior toward persons should be considered when evaluating conduct.

---

## 8. Research Integrity

Behavioral conduct and scientific validity are related but distinct.

This Code does not establish a complete research-integrity framework.

However, participants must not knowingly present fabricated, falsified, plagiarized, or deliberately misrepresented material as legitimate scientific evidence.

Questions involving fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, authorship, research records, conflicts of interest, data integrity, methodological misconduct, or other forms of scientific misconduct may be governed by dedicated Architektonia research-integrity policies and procedures.

Scientific error is not automatically scientific misconduct.

**Being wrong is compatible with science.**

Deliberately corrupting the process by which truth is investigated is not.

---

## 9. Confidentiality and Protected Information

Participation in a research environment does not authorize the disclosure of protected information.

Participants must respect applicable boundaries concerning:

- confidential research data;
- personal information;
- restricted institutional information;
- unpublished protected research;
- credentials and authentication material;
- security-sensitive information;
- proprietary information;
- protected technological foundations;
- BT; and
- BT-enabling information.

Public scientific discussion must not be used as a mechanism to circumvent legitimate confidentiality, security, intellectual-property, or publication controls.

Applicable licensing and intellectual-property principles are defined separately in `LICENSING.md`.

---

## 10. AI-Assisted Participation

Architektonia recognizes that artificial intelligence may assist research, writing, analysis, programming, simulation, exploration, and scientific discussion.

The use of AI assistance does not transfer responsibility away from the human participant submitting the contribution.

```text
AI ASSISTANCE
      ≠
TRANSFER OF RESPONSIBILITY
```

A human participant remains responsible for the claims, representations, citations, code, data, and actions submitted under that participant's authority.

AI-generated or AI-assisted material should therefore be subjected to appropriate human review before it is presented as a contribution.

Where the use of AI is materially relevant to the interpretation, reproducibility, provenance, or evaluation of a scientific contribution, appropriate disclosure is expected.

Architektonia strongly encourages contributors using artificial intelligence to become familiar with the **AICL Scale — AI Content Level Framework** as a common framework for describing human–AI contribution.

For institutionalized Architektonia publications in which AI has materially contributed to the work, an appropriate AICL declaration should accompany the publication unless a more specific Architektonia policy applies.

AICL disclosure describes the contribution process.

It does not establish the scientific validity, authority, or quality of the resulting work.

```text
AI CONTRIBUTION
      ↓
DISCLOSURE
      ↓
HUMAN ACCOUNTABILITY
      ↓
SCIENTIFIC EVALUATION
```

The mere use of AI does not establish the truth, authority, or scientific validity of a claim.

---

## 11. Authority and Scientific Criticism

Institutional authority and scientific truth are not equivalent.

No founder, director, researcher, maintainer, contributor, reviewer, artificial agent, or institutional body possesses authority to make a scientific proposition true merely by declaring it so.

Likewise, institutional roles must not be used to shield scientific claims from legitimate examination.

```text
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
          ≠
SCIENTIFIC VALIDITY
```

Institutional decisions may determine what Architektonia does.

Evidence and scientific reasoning determine whether scientific claims withstand examination.

---

## 12. Reporting

Conduct concerns should be reported through the mechanism designated by the Institute.

Reports should, where reasonably possible, identify:

- the behavior being reported;
- relevant context;
- available evidence;
- affected participants;
- relevant dates or interactions; and
- immediate safety, confidentiality, or research-integrity concerns.

Reports should be handled with appropriate discretion.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may themselves constitute misconduct.

Good-faith reports must not result in retaliation.

Until a dedicated reporting channel is established, sensitive conduct reports should **not** be submitted through public GitHub issues.

# 13. Enforcement, Repair, and Reintegration

## 13.1 Purpose of Enforcement

The purpose of enforcement at Architektonia is not punishment.

It is to restore or protect the conditions necessary for truthful, rigorous, and humane scientific work.

Architektonia seeks, whenever reasonably possible, to preserve:

```text
TRUTH
│
│  Establish what happened.
│
PERSON
│
│  Preserve human dignity.
│
RELATION
│
│  Repair scientific cooperation when possible.
│
BOUNDARY
│
│  Protect the research environment when necessary.
│
▼
CONTINUATION OF SCIENTIFIC WORK
```

Architektonia should neither preserve participation at the expense of the research environment nor terminate participation merely because conflict has occurred.

The preferred objective is:

> **Repair with adequate protection.**

---

## 13.2 General Process and Institutional Boundary

When conduct appears inconsistent with this Code, the normal process is:

```text
OBSERVATION
      ↓
CLARIFICATION
      ↓
DIALOGUE
      ↓
REPAIR
      ↓
BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
      ↓
RESTRICTION
```

These stages are not automatic punishments.

They represent progressively stronger institutional responses as understanding increases and as the conditions required for continued scientific cooperation become clearer.

However, Architektonia's repair framework applies only where the situation still involves at least the **minimal emerging presence of an architecture of communion**: a relationship in which some meaningful possibility of shared truth-seeking, recognition, cooperation, repair, or renewed participation remains present.

```text
MINIMAL EMERGING
ARCHITECTURE OF COMMUNION
          │
          ├── present ──► ARCHITEKTONIA REPAIR FRAMEWORK
          │
          └── absent ───► REFER BEYOND THE FRAMEWORK
```

Conduct falling outside this domain should not be artificially transformed into an Architektonia repair process.

Where appropriate, matters involving criminal conduct, immediate safety, professional misconduct, serious behavioral concerns, legal obligations, or matters requiring specialized authority should be referred to competent external authorities or appropriately qualified professionals.

**Architektonia should not exercise authority that properly belongs elsewhere.**

Immediate protective measures may nevertheless be taken within Architektonia's own research environment when necessary to protect participants, information, systems, or research activities.

---

## 13.3 Observation

The first responsibility is to determine what actually occurred.

Observation should distinguish, as far as reasonably possible:

```text
OBSERVATION
      ≠
INTERPRETATION
      ≠
HYPOTHESIS
      ≠
JUDGMENT
```

Relevant evidence should be preserved.

Where appropriate, multiple perspectives should be considered.

Scientific disagreement must not be mistaken for misconduct merely because the disagreement is strong.

Likewise, harmful conduct must not be excused merely because it occurred during scientific disagreement.

---

## 13.4 Clarification

Before drawing conclusions, the Institute should seek clarification where circumstances permit.

Clarification may establish:

- what was said or done;
- what was intended;
- what was understood by others;
- what consequences occurred;
- whether relevant context is missing;
- whether a misunderstanding exists;
- whether the conduct is isolated or recurring; and
- whether an immediate protective boundary is required.

**Clarification is an epistemic step before it is a disciplinary one.**

---

## 13.5 Dialogue

Where appropriate and safe, participants should be given an opportunity to explain their understanding of the situation.

Dialogue is not intended to force agreement.

Its purpose is to make truth, responsibility, misunderstanding, harm, and possible repair more visible.

No participant should be required to abandon a good-faith scientific position as a condition of interpersonal reconciliation.

```text
RECONCILIATION
      ≠
SCIENTIFIC AGREEMENT
```

---

## 13.6 Repair

Where a relationship or research environment has been damaged, **Architektonia contributors should seek proportionate repair when reasonably possible**.

Repair may include:

- acknowledgment;
- clarification;
- correction;
- withdrawal or amendment of inappropriate material;
- restoration of proper attribution;
- correction of the scientific record;
- apology;
- restoration of appropriate communication;
- agreed behavioral commitments;
- procedural changes; or
- other actions directly related to the harm or dysfunction identified.

Repair should address the actual problem rather than merely produce symbolic compliance.

At the same time, Architektonia recognizes that **symbolic compliance may itself constitute an emerging repair architecture**.

```text
SYMBOLIC COMPLIANCE
        │
        ▼
may constitute
        │
        ▼
EMERGING REPAIR ARCHITECTURE
        │
        ▼
but does not necessarily constitute
        │
        ▼
COMPLETED REPAIR
```

A symbolic act may therefore be meaningful when it represents an observable first movement toward acknowledgment, renewed cooperation, restored trust, or a more substantive process of repair.

---

## 13.7 Boundary and Protection

Repair does not eliminate the need for boundaries.

A boundary establishes conditions under which scientific cooperation can responsibly continue.

Possible protective measures may include:

- communication boundaries;
- review requirements;
- limited repository permissions;
- supervision;
- temporary separation of participants;
- restrictions on particular activities;
- additional approval requirements; or
- other proportionate safeguards.

```text
BOUNDARY
      ≠
PUNISHMENT

BOUNDARY
      =
CONDITION FOR RESPONSIBLE CONTINUATION
```

Boundaries should be no broader or longer than reasonably necessary for their protective purpose.

---

## 13.8 Restriction

When ordinary boundaries are insufficient, participation may be formally restricted.

Restriction may concern:

- repository access;
- ability to submit changes;
- review privileges;
- discussion privileges;
- access to particular research activities;
- access to protected information; or
- other defined forms of participation.

A restriction should, where reasonably possible, identify:

```text
WHY
WHAT
DURATION
CONDITIONS
REVIEW POINT
PATH TO RETURN
```

Restriction should therefore normally create a **rehabilitation path**, not an undefined state of institutional suspension.

---

## 13.9 First Loop — Rehabilitation

A participant under restriction may enter a rehabilitation process.

The purpose of rehabilitation is to determine whether conditions for responsible and constructive participation can be restored.

```text
RESTRICTION
      ↓
REHABILITATION
      ↓
EVALUATION
      ↓
BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
      ↓
RESTORED PARTICIPATION
```

Rehabilitation may involve:

- demonstrated understanding of the issue;
- fulfillment of agreed corrective actions;
- restoration of trust through observable conduct;
- additional review or supervision;
- temporary safeguards; or
- other conditions proportionate to the original problem.

Whenever appropriate, the participant should be invited to contribute to the design of their own rehabilitation process.

This may include identifying causes, proposing safeguards, defining observable commitments, suggesting measures of restored trust, and identifying conditions that could support successful participation.

```text
PARTICIPANT
     +
INSTITUTION
     ↓
CO-DESIGN
     ↓
PROPOSED REHABILITATION ARCHITECTURE
     ↓
REVIEW
     ↓
APPROVAL WHEN REQUIRED
     ↓
IMPLEMENTATION
```

For serious, repeated, or higher-risk conduct, the proposed rehabilitation architecture may require approval by the competent institutional authority and may include independent or specialized professional input.

Successful rehabilitation does not necessarily require immediate removal of every boundary.

Participation may resume within an appropriate protective architecture.

---

## 13.10 Exclusion

When the research environment cannot be adequately protected through lesser measures, a participant may be excluded.

Exclusion is a protective institutional act.

It may be necessary when conduct is severe, persistent, deliberately harmful, incompatible with responsible participation, or repeatedly resistant to reasonable repair.

```text
EXCLUSION
      ≠
DECLARATION OF HUMAN WORTH

EXCLUSION
      =
WITHDRAWAL OF PARTICIPATION
TO PROTECT THE RESEARCH ENVIRONMENT
```

The dignity of the excluded person remains unchanged.

---

## 13.11 Second Loop — Reintegration

Exclusion need not automatically be permanent.

Where appropriate, an excluded participant may later request or be offered a reintegration process.

```text
EXCLUSION
      ↓
REINTEGRATION PROCESS
      ↓
EVALUATION
      ↓
BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
      ↓
CONDITIONAL PARTICIPATION
      ↓
RESTORED PARTICIPATION
```

Reintegration should evaluate present conditions rather than merely repeat the original judgment.

Relevant considerations may include:

- the nature and severity of the original conduct;
- evidence of understanding;
- subsequent conduct;
- corrective actions;
- time elapsed;
- risks to participants or research activities;
- ability to respect necessary boundaries; and
- reasonable evidence that renewed participation can occur responsibly.

Reintegration may be gradual and conditional.

---

## 13.12 Re-Exclusion

A participant who has been reintegrated may be excluded again if renewed conduct makes continued participation incompatible with adequate protection of the research environment.

Re-exclusion should not automatically establish that future reintegration is impossible.

However, repeated failure after reintegration constitutes relevant evidence for future risk assessment.

Institutional memory therefore matters.

```text
FORGIVENESS
      ≠
AMNESIA

PAST CONDUCT
      ≠
PERMANENT IDENTITY

PAST CONDUCT
      =
RELEVANT EVIDENCE
```

---

## 13.13 Third Loop — Hardened Reintegration

Multiple re-exclusions indicate that the previous reintegration architecture may have been insufficient.

The appropriate response is therefore not merely to repeat the same process.

The reintegration process should become more demanding as evidence of repeated failure accumulates.

```text
MULTIPLE RE-EXCLUSIONS
          ↓
HARDENED REINTEGRATION PROCESS
          ↓
STRONGER EVIDENTIARY REQUIREMENTS
          +
STRONGER BOUNDARIES
          +
GREATER OVERSIGHT
          +
MORE GRADUAL RESTORATION
          ↓
BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
          ↓
CONDITIONAL PARTICIPATION
```

Possible measures may include:

- longer observation periods;
- narrower initial permissions;
- stronger supervision;
- staged restoration of access;
- explicit behavioral commitments;
- independent review;
- lower intervention thresholds for recurrence of previously identified unacceptable conduct;
- higher evidence requirements for restored trust; or
- longer intervals before reconsideration.

The objective remains reintegration **when reintegration can reasonably occur without sacrificing the integrity or safety of the research environment**.

```text
FIRST OCCURRENCE
      ↓
little prior evidence
      ↓
ordinary intervention threshold


REPEATED OCCURRENCE
      ↓
accumulated evidence
      ↓
lower threshold for protective intervention
```

---

## 13.14 Restricted and Supported Participation

Responsible participation need not always be treated as a binary choice between full participation and exclusion.

Where recurring or serious behavior prevents responsible participation in some areas of the Institute but does not require complete exclusion, Architektonia may establish a **restricted or supported participation architecture**.

Participation may be limited to specific repositories, research activities, communication channels, roles, permissions, or forms of collaboration.

Where the behavior involved requires competencies beyond those reasonably held by Institute contributors or maintainers, continued participation may be conditioned on the involvement of appropriately qualified professionals.

```text
PARTICIPATION
      ↓
RISK / CAPABILITY ASSESSMENT
      ↓
┌──────────────┬──────────────────┬───────────────┐
│              │                  │               │
FULL       RESTRICTED          SUPPORTED      EXCLUDED
ACCESS     PARTICIPATION       PARTICIPATION
│              │                  │
│              │                  └── qualified professional
│              │                      involvement where needed
└──────────────┴──────────────────┘
              ↓
      PERIODIC REASSESSMENT
```

Such restrictions should be based on the requirements of the research environment and the participant's demonstrated capacity for responsible participation in the relevant context.

They should not be interpreted as declarations about the participant's intrinsic worth or permanent capacity for change.

---

## 13.15 Institutional Limits

Architektonia is committed to the possibility of repair.

It is not required to expose participants, research activities, protected information, systems, or the Institute itself to unreasonable recurring risk.

There may therefore be circumstances in which available evidence does not support another attempt at reintegration.

```text
OPENNESS TO REPAIR
        +
MEMORY OF EXPERIENCE
        +
PROPORTIONATE PROTECTION
        =
RESPONSIBLE REINTEGRATION
```

A decision not to reopen participation should be based on the protection required by the research environment, not on a declaration that a person is incapable of change.

Future reconsideration may remain possible when materially new evidence or circumstances arise.

---

## 13.16 Escalation Is Not Automatic

The processes described above are not a mechanical ladder.

Not every incident requires every stage.

A minor incident may be resolved through clarification.

A misunderstanding may require no restriction.

A serious situation may require immediate protection or referral to competent authorities.

A repeated pattern may justify stronger boundaries than an isolated event.

Institutional responses should therefore be:

- evidence-based;
- proportionate;
- context-sensitive;
- attentive to recurrence;
- attentive to conflicts of interest;
- respectful of confidentiality;
- documented where appropriate;
- revisable when materially new evidence appears; and
- independent, as far as reasonably possible, from the scientific position under dispute.

---

## 13.17 Scientific Disagreement Must Remain Protected

None of these enforcement mechanisms may be used to suppress legitimate scientific disagreement.

A participant must not be restricted, excluded, or subjected to a rehabilitation or reintegration process merely for:

- challenging a claim;
- challenging evidence;
- challenging a method;
- challenging a model;
- challenging an inference;
- challenging a conclusion;
- requesting evidence;
- attempting replication;
- presenting contradictory evidence;
- identifying an error;
- proposing an alternative explanation; or
- disagreeing in good faith with Architektonia or any person holding authority within it.

```text
SCIENTIFIC CONTRADICTION
          ≠
MISCONDUCT
```

---

## 13.18 The Three Repair Loops

The Architektonia repair architecture can therefore be summarized as:

```text
OBSERVATION
      ↓
CLARIFICATION
      ↓
DIALOGUE
      ↓
REPAIR
      ↓
BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
      ↓
RESTRICTION
      │
      └──► REHABILITATION
                ↓
             CO-DESIGN
                ↓
         BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
                ↓
         RESTORED PARTICIPATION


EXCLUSION
      │
      └──► REINTEGRATION PROCESS
                ↓
         BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
                ↓
         CONDITIONAL / RESTORED PARTICIPATION


MULTIPLE RE-EXCLUSIONS
      │
      └──► HARDENED REINTEGRATION PROCESS
                ↓
         STRONGER BOUNDARY / PROTECTION
                ↓
         CONDITIONAL / RESTORED PARTICIPATION
```

Each loop retains institutional memory.

Each loop allows new evidence.

Each loop preserves the possibility of repair.

Each loop may strengthen protection when previous protection proved insufficient.

> **Seek truth first. Repair what can be repaired. Protect what must be protected. Preserve the dignity of every person throughout the process.**

# 14. Post-Resolution Contribution to Repair Research

Architektonia scientifically studies the emergence, transformation, stabilization, maturation, repair, and disorganization of human communion.

Completed disagreement and repair processes may therefore eventually provide valuable observations for the scientific study of **architectures of repair of human communion**.

However, a conduct, disagreement, rehabilitation, or reintegration process is **not automatically a research process**.

Its first purpose is resolution, repair, reintegration, or protection.

Only **after the resolution process has been completed and, where applicable, the participant has been reintegrated**, may the persons involved voluntarily decide to propose the completed process for scientific study.

```text
CONFLICT / RUPTURE
        ↓
RESOLUTION PROCESS
        ↓
REPAIR / REHABILITATION / REINTEGRATION
        ↓
PROCESS COMPLETED
        │
        │ no research obligation
        ▼
VOLUNTARY RESEARCH PROPOSAL
        ↓
CONSENT OF ALL INVOLVED CONTRIBUTORS
        ↓
RESEARCH / ETHICS QUALIFICATION
        ↓
RESEARCH DATA
        ↓
ANONYMIZED RESULTS
```

Participation in such research must never be a condition of rehabilitation, reintegration, restoration of repository access, restoration of institutional standing, or any other favorable decision under this Code.

```text
REINTEGRATION
      ≠
CONSENT TO RESEARCH
```

---

## 14.1 Consent of All Involved Contributors

A completed resolution process may be admitted into research only with the informed and voluntary consent of **all contributors whose participation, conduct, communications, testimony, or identifiable experience would form part of the proposed research material**.

The consent of one participant cannot substitute for the consent of another.

```text
A consents       ✓
B consents       ✓
C consents       ✓
                 ↓
        RESEARCH MAY PROCEED


A consents       ✓
B consents       ✓
C declines       ✗
                 ↓
        PROCESS NOT ADMITTED
        IN THAT FORM
```

Declining research participation must not be interpreted as failure of repair, lack of cooperation, insufficient goodwill, or evidence against successful rehabilitation or reintegration.

---

## 14.2 Consent Free From Social Pressure

Consent must be free from **social, institutional, professional, relational, or reputational pressure**.

No participant should be pressured to consent because other participants have already consented, because the research may benefit the Institute, or because participation is perceived as desirable by colleagues, researchers, supervisors, maintainers, or institutional authorities.

```text
REPAIR
      ≠
RESEARCH PARTICIPATION

REINTEGRATION
      ≠
RESEARCH PARTICIPATION

GOODWILL
      ≠
CONSENT TO RESEARCH
```

Any credible sign that consent may have been influenced by social pressure triggers the **immediate suspension and removal of the affected research data from active research use**.

```text
SIGN OF SOCIAL PRESSURE
          ↓
IMMEDIATE DATA SUSPENSION
          ↓
REMOVAL FROM ACTIVE RESEARCH
          ↓
CONSENT INTEGRITY REVIEW
          ↓
     ┌────┴────┐
     │         │
CONSENT      CONSENT
VALID        COMPROMISED
     │         │
     ▼         ▼
possible     DATA REMAINS
readmission  EXCLUDED
```

During review, the affected data must not be analyzed further, used to support new scientific conclusions, included in new research outputs, or submitted for publication.

If consent is found to have been compromised, the affected research data must remain excluded.

If free and informed consent can later be independently established, any readmission of the data constitutes a **new research admission decision**.

Previous consent must not simply be presumed to have revived.

Where the integrity of consent cannot be established with sufficient confidence:

```text
UNCERTAIN CONSENT
      ↓
DO NOT USE THE DATA
```

No scientific value, rarity of the case, amount of work already invested, or importance of expected findings overrides this requirement.

> **Scientific value cannot override voluntary consent.**

---

## 14.3 Right to Withdraw Before Publication

Any consenting participant may withdraw consent **at any time before publication of the research results**.

A single withdrawal is sufficient to stop use of the resolution process in the form requiring that participant's consent.

```text
UNANIMOUS CONSENT
        ↓
RESEARCH
        ↓
ANALYSIS
        ↓
DRAFT RESULTS
        │
        ├── consent maintained ──► PUBLICATION
        │
        └── one withdrawal ──────► STOP / REMOVE /
                                    REQUALIFY DATA
```

Where consent is withdrawn, affected material must be removed from active research use or the research must be redesigned so that the withdrawing participant's protected contribution is no longer used, subject to applicable ethical, legal, and research-record requirements.

The applicable research protocol should define how withdrawal affects derived analyses, aggregated data, intermediate research artifacts, and records that must legitimately be retained.

---

## 14.4 Publication Boundary

Once results have been publicly released, Architektonia cannot guarantee the removal of copies already disseminated, cited, downloaded, archived, or independently retained by third parties.

Participants should therefore be clearly informed of the publication boundary before giving consent.

A meaningful opportunity to withdraw must exist before that boundary is crossed.

```text
BEFORE PUBLICATION
        │
        └── WITHDRAWAL POSSIBLE

PUBLICATION
        │
        ▼
PUBLIC DISSEMINATION BOUNDARY
```

Any additional rights required by applicable research ethics, privacy law, consent agreements, or institutional policy remain applicable.

---

## 14.5 Anonymization

Research results derived from disagreement, repair, rehabilitation, or reintegration processes must be **anonymized before publication**, unless a separate explicit research protocol establishes a justified and voluntarily consented exception.

Published results should not reveal participant identities through direct identifiers or reasonably foreseeable combinations of contextual information.

Anonymization may therefore require protection against identification through:

- names;
- usernames;
- repository identifiers;
- roles;
- dates;
- quotations;
- unusual events;
- technical details;
- institutional positions;
- demographic information;
- contextual combinations; or
- other reasonably identifying information.

```text
RESEARCH RECORD
      ↓
ANONYMIZATION
      ↓
RE-IDENTIFICATION REVIEW
      ↓
PUBLICATION REVIEW
      ↓
PUBLIC SCIENTIFIC RESULTS
```

Where meaningful anonymization cannot reasonably be achieved, the material should not be publicly released through this pathway.

---

## 14.6 Separation of Institutional Functions

Architektonia maintains a distinction between:

```text
CONDUCT PROCESS
      │
      └── resolve / repair / protect


REHABILITATION / REINTEGRATION
      │
      └── restore responsible participation


RESEARCH PROCESS
      │
      └── scientifically study repair architectures


PUBLICATION PROCESS
      │
      └── determine what may enter the public record
```

Authority exercised in one process does not automatically confer authority in another.

The scientific value of a repair process must not influence whether a participant receives fair treatment in the conduct, rehabilitation, or reintegration process.

Likewise, successful repair or reintegration does not create an obligation to become a research participant.

> **Repair the relationship first. Study the repair only afterward, freely, with the consent of everyone involved.**

## 15. Scope

This Code governs participation in research activities conducted **within or through repositories of the Architektonia Research Institute**.

It applies to any person who contributes to, reviews, discusses, develops, evaluates, reproduces, tests, maintains, or otherwise participates in research work associated with an Architektonia repository.

This includes interactions occurring through:

- commits and proposed changes;
- issues;
- pull requests;
- reviews;
- discussions;
- scientific critiques;
- research documentation;
- datasets;
- models and simulations;
- software and computational experiments; and
- other collaborative activities directly associated with an Architektonia repository.

The Code applies whether the participant is an Institute researcher, employee, collaborator, external researcher, reviewer, developer, student, invited contributor, or member of the public contributing to an Architektonia research activity.

Its purpose is to establish the conditions under which people can work together in the pursuit of scientific truth:

```text
RESEARCH ACTIVITY
        ↓
FEARLESS EXAMINATION OF IDEAS
        +
RIGOROUS SCIENTIFIC PRACTICE
        +
REMARKABLE KINDNESS TO PERSONS
```

Participation in an Architektonia repository constitutes participation in a shared research environment and is therefore subject to this Code for the duration and scope of that participation.

---

## 16. Interpretation

This Code should be interpreted in a manner that preserves both scientific freedom and human dignity.

When tension arises between vigorous criticism and respectful conduct, the objective should not be to weaken scientific examination.

The objective should be to direct that examination toward the **idea, evidence, method, model, inference, or claim** rather than toward the human worth of the person presenting it.

The Institute's cultural expectation can therefore be summarized as:

```text
BE FEARLESS
IN THE SEARCH FOR TRUTH.

BE PRECISE
IN CRITICISM.

BE HUMBLE
ABOUT WHAT IS KNOWN.

BE REMARKABLY KIND
TO PEOPLE.
```
